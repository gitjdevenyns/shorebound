02 · The specimen

The product is the proof, not the pitch

Shorebound is a researched, offline-first shore-fishing guide for a stretch of Florida's Gulf coast. Live, with a database, four serverless functions, twelve migrations and a revenue plan. Small enough to audit completely; real enough that the findings cost something.

04 · The method

The discipline is in the brief, not the tooling

Anyone can open fifteen chat windows. What turns that into a review board is what each agent is handed before it runs.

The hard constraints, restated

Every brief carries the rules the product cannot break — offline-first, no secrets in the bundle, location never leaves the device — so no agent rediscovers them expensively.

The mistakes already made

A record of past failures goes in the brief. An agent that repeats a documented mistake is a brief problem, not a model problem.

Cite file and line

A finding without a location is an opinion. Requiring the citation makes the report checkable instead of persuasive.

A stated boundary

Reviewers may not fix. The agent that finds a problem never grades its own repair.

05 · The roster · 1 of 13
architectran

The only role whose job is the shape of the whole system rather than one slice of it.

Purpose

Turn the brief's goals into a coherent, sequenced design and keep every other agent pulling the same direction. It protects that vantage point by staying out of implementation it can delegate.

How it works

Reads brief, roadmap and past mistakes before proposing anything. Produces design plus a scoped task breakdown, then delegates by name. May not pick a vendor from memory.

Why you need it

Specialists optimise their own slice and nobody owns the seams. Five of its six critical findings were seam problems — a rename that moved the app but not the deploy gate, an account gate bolted onto an offline-first product without anyone asking what it does with no signal. No single specialist would have been looking there.

05 · The roster · 2 of 13
backend-engineerran

Implements what was decided without re-deciding it along the way.

Purpose

Turn an approved architecture into working, verified backend code — and flag clearly when the decision does not hold up once you are implementing against it.

How it works

Reads every migration in timestamp order. Audits row-level security table by table, and authorization at every function entry point, asking who can call what.

Why you need it

Database permissions fail silently and in both directions. It found both production vulnerabilities. One line — revoking writes but never reads — simultaneously exposed advertiser data to the public and broke the owner's own save button. Nothing surfaced either; there was no error to notice.

05 · The roster · 3 of 13
qa-reviewerran

The check that nothing is marked done on a claim nobody verified.

Purpose

Independence from whoever built the thing. It re-runs and re-checks rather than summarising what it was told.

How it works

Runs the build and the suite itself. Hunts specifically for tests that would still pass if the feature were deleted, and for claims in the docs the code does not support.

Why you need it

A builder cannot see the assumption it built on. It found the product inventing the content it sells itself on being researched — then enumerated all 104 cases and named the seven resolving by array index. A summary would have said “recipes render correctly”, and been true.

05 · The roster · 4 of 13
security-privacy-reviewerran

Makes the product's trust commitments true in the code, not just in the copy.

Purpose

Organise posture against a real standard — NIST CSF 2.0 — so gaps are visible and prioritised, rather than discovered one incident at a time.

How it works

Verifies against the running system, not the source. Names what needs a lawyer or a penetration tester instead of answering it with a confident tone.

Why you need it

“We do not store that” is a claim, and claims rot. It issued a real request with the public key and got nine rows of private advertiser data back. Later it audited a package I had installed, verified the exact bytes that executed, and found a path-traversal write nobody had looked for.

05 · The roster · 5 of 13
test-engineerran

Makes regressions impossible to ship silently.

Purpose

Concentrate coverage where this product is most likely to break in ways that matter, rather than wherever testing is easiest.

How it works

Maps the untested surface and ranks gaps by what breaking would cost — not by coverage percentage, which rewards the easy half.

Why you need it

A green suite is not evidence; it is a sample. Behind 316 passing tests it found the entire live-data parsing layer untested — 533 lines including timezone handling across daylight-saving boundaries. Every test passed. None of them looked there.

05 · The roster · 6 of 13
documentation-writerran

Keeps documentation a trustworthy record of what is true right now.

Purpose

So a new engineer, an investor, or a future version of this swarm can rely on the docs instead of re-deriving the state from the code.

How it works

Marks every factual claim CONFIRMED or DRIFTED against the repository. Counts things itself rather than repeating a number.

Why you need it

Numbers in documents get repeated until they become facts. It disproved two the product was selling on: “20 verified businesses” was 9 of 20, and “159 cited sources” could not be reproduced by any counting method. Both were already in sales material.

05 · The roster · 7 of 13
project-managerran

Guards scope and reports honest status. It says no to good ideas.

Purpose

Named for this project's actual failure mode: not laziness, appetite. It builds excellent things that were not the next thing. This role notices that out loud.

How it works

Re-measures every metric rather than carrying it forward; each carries a verified flag and the method used to get it.

Why you need it

“Fixed” and “shipped” are different claims, and only one of them protects anyone. Seven fixes were reported done. It queried the live database directly and found two had never been applied — the code was correct and the vulnerabilities were still live.

05 · The roster · 8 of 13
ui-designerran

Makes the product's differentiation visible in the interface.

Purpose

If the moat is that the product shows its reasoning, the interface must make the why legible at a glance rather than burying it behind a generic list.

How it works

Drives a real browser at real viewport sizes and measures rather than eyeballs — screenfuls, tap targets, pixel heights.

Why you need it

Interface defects are invisible in source and obvious in a screenshot. Five of its eight critical findings could not be read in the code: raw exception text printed on the flagship screen, a signed-out navigation where ten links did nothing, and a page measuring 13.5 screenfuls with no way to move around it.

05 · The roster · 9 of 13
ui-designran

Designs and builds the visual work, and verifies it before reporting.

Purpose

Exists because earlier design rounds were rejected outright. Its brief names that failure and raises the bar rather than pretending it did not happen.

How it works

Works in a live design surface, not in prose. Screenshots at phone and desktop before it claims anything is done.

Why you need it

Design described in text is not design. Eight files this run — a 16-slide deck, a duplex one-pager, a three-sheet proposal, welcome screens at two sizes, and three distinct interface directions built for comparison. It also preserved two deliberate blanks it was told not to fill.

05 · The roster · 10 of 13
infra-cost-strategistran

The standing check against cost creep and cheap-now, expensive-later lock-in.

Purpose

Keep running costs proportionate to revenue without the architect or the engineers having to become vendor-pricing experts.

How it works

Costs options at realistic scale and checks commercial terms before price. Never signs anything — spend is a human action.

Why you need it

Engineers pick vendors on ergonomics and discover the terms at launch. Asked why the map looked dated, it found the real problem was that neither tile source was licensed for a commercial app — and that the free-forever option was also the only one that worked offline.

05 · The roster · 11 of 13
marketing-creativeran

Writes the outward-facing voice under a binding claims policy.

Purpose

Draft, never publish. Approval is a human act, because a wrong post is not a bug you can revert — it has already been seen and indexed.

How it works

Separates the product's own voice, which may be rewritten, from researched content, which may only be quoted. Paraphrasing research is writing new research.

Why you need it

Instructions get pasted in as finished copy. It found the owner's spoken tone-direction shipped verbatim on four pages, and a maintainer's work order — “re-check these whenever the photo is replaced” — rendering under every species photograph in production.

05 · The roster · 12 of 13
product-marketingran

Keeps every claim accountable to build state rather than roadmap intentions.

Purpose

Make the real differentiation legible to an outsider in one sentence — without inventing capability the product does not have.

How it works

Traces each claim to something checkable and refuses the ones it cannot source, rather than softening them.

Why you need it

Marketing is where unverified claims enter a company permanently. It wrote this deck — and refused to attribute one finding because no file records who made it, flagged two authorship claims rather than asserting them, and declined to claim the method is novel after checking the field.

05 · The roster · 13 of 13
naming-brand-researcherran

No name reaches external material without an honest account of what was screened.

Purpose

Speed of naming is not the priority. Avoiding a costly rename or a trademark conflict after launch is.

How it works

Live collision testing, domain checks, and category-adjacency judgement — how close the colliding use is, not merely whether the string exists.

Why you need it

A name is the most expensive thing to change late. It killed three previously-favoured names on evidence, including one recommended twice before anyone ran the test. Renaming touches the router, the service worker and every piece of marketing.

06 · Disagreement

Convergence is evidence. Contradiction is the interesting part.

Three lanes, one finding, no coordination

Architecture, security and QA each arrived by different routes at the same fact: production deploys through a path where none of the checks run. Three independent confirmations beat one confident report.

Three reviews were wrong about the same thing

All three cited a test as proof image licensing was handled. That test only examines images that already declare a licence — so the seventeen with none were invisible to it.

The fix was itself defective

Opening the app to offline readers was right, but the check was live: one bar of signal and the reader was thrown back to a sign-in form mid-page. Caught and corrected the same day.

And a person decides

Every report ends with the calls only a human can make. The account gate was flagged as a decision about the sign-up funnel, not an engineering one, and left open deliberately.

07 · Limits

What I have learned not to trust it with

  • Two more agents exist that this deck does not show — including an unattended one, designed to work with nobody watching. Neither has ever executed, so neither earned a slide, and the unattended one does not go near a client repository until its safety rules have been tested rather than merely written.
  • One agent's declared tools do not exist. Its definition names three interfaces the server does not expose. It reviews well; it has never built.
  • A migration was built and then reverted. A correctly-licensed vector basemap compiled, passed 346 tests, put every marker in the right geography — and never drew. Parked with the diagnosis written down rather than shipped or deleted.
  • Agents inherit assumptions from wherever they came from. Eight definitions still carried a different product's domain reasoning phrased as instruction — a marketing agent advising on real-estate aggregation. The first commit strips it back out.
  • Reviewers are confidently wrong in packs. Independent review reduces that risk. It does not remove it, and nothing replaces checking the thing yourself.
08 · Transfer

What this looks like at your company

Multi-agent review is not novel in 2026 and I will not claim it is. The differentiation is in the briefs, the constraints and the verification — the part that does not come out of the box.

  • Parallel independent review, not a chain — specialists that cannot see each other's findings before filing their own.
  • Every finding cites a file and a line, so the report is checkable rather than persuasive.
  • Reviewers may not fix. Nobody grades their own repair.
  • Escalation paths that leave the system — legal, licensing and adversarial testing routed to humans by name.
  • A fix in the working tree is not a fix in production until it has been checked against the running system.
Close

The judgement is the deliverable

The models are a commodity and they get better without me. What does not come out of the box is knowing what to hand an agent, what to withhold, how to make its findings checkable, and when to stop and get a human. That is what I would bring.
