# Shop web-presence audit

Researched 24 Aug 2026 against `src/data/shops.ts`. Research only — no business was
contacted. Every technical finding below came from fetching the shop's own site
and reading its headers, HTML, TLS certificate and XML sitemap.

## Method and its limits — read this first

**Verified first-party:** HTTPS status and certificate expiry (`openssl s_client`),
mobile responsiveness (presence of a `viewport` meta tag *and* `@media` queries in
the actual stylesheets — a viewport tag alone proves nothing), last-updated dates
(HTTP `Last-Modified` headers and `<lastmod>` values in XML sitemaps, which are far
harder to fake than a footer year), platform/CMS (generator meta tags), published
hours, published bait, and outbound social links.

**NOT verified — treat as an open gap:** Google Business Profile presence, star
ratings, review counts, and most-recent-social-post dates. The session's web-search
budget was exhausted before these could be checked, and Google, Facebook and
DuckDuckGo all refuse direct fetching. **No rating or review count appears anywhere
in this document, because none could be confirmed.** Closing this gap is roughly
30 minutes of manual searching and should happen before any shop is approached.

**A note on footer copyright years.** Six sites display "© 2026". In every case the
year is generated by script or template, not typed by a human — Island Discount
Tackle literally emits `document.write((new Date()).getFullYear())`. Footer years
were ignored. Sitemap and `Last-Modified` dates were used instead.

`src/data/shops.ts` also now contains an `EXAMPLE-osprey-point` placeholder entry
(example.com, 941-555-0142). It is not a real business and is excluded throughout.

Can a customer learn today's live-shrimp status? **No** — the FAQ names a second

**Assessment: adequate site.**

## Assessment

### Category counts

Of the **18 independent businesses** (excluding Safe Harbor and the city marina):

| Category | Count | Shops |
|---|---|---|
| **No web presence at all** | 1 | Mitch's Bait N Tackle |
| **Social only** | 0 verified | — (unverifiable; see method note) |
| **Outdated or broken site** | 6 | 4th Street Tackle, Island Discount Tackle, Fishin' Frank's, Gator Jim's (×2 locations, 1 owner), Palmetto Bait and Tackle |
| **Adequate site** | 8 | Bridge Street, Cortez, O'Neill's, Riviera, Gandy, New Pass, CB's, Gasparilla |
| **Good site, no need** | 3 | Fishermen's Headquarters, Skyway Bait & Tackle, Economy Tackle |

Plus 2 chain/municipal, both with good sites and both out of scope.

### How many are genuinely underserved?

**Seven shops, but only six prospects** — Gator Jim's two entries are one owner.

Ranked by clarity of need:

1. **4th Street Tackle** — expired certificate, browser security warning, untouched
   since 2021, separate mobile subdomain over plain HTTP.
2. **Mitch's Bait N Tackle** — nothing at all. No domain, no first-party anything.
3. **Island Discount Tackle** — not mobile responsive, true copyright meta of
   2006-2014, an opening time with no closing time.
4. **Fishin' Frank's** — FrontPage HTML, entirely non-responsive, but weekly-updated
   content that is better than anyone else's.

Behind those, Gator Jim's needs a content refresh rather than a build, and Palmetto
Bait and Tackle needs hours added to a site that has otherwise sat still since 2024.

### The gap that actually matters

**Not one of the twenty sites can tell an angler whether live shrimp is in the tank
right now.** Every one is a static brochure. The best any of them manages is Economy
Tackle's permanent "Live Bait Available: Shrimp and Worms" banner — a standing claim,
not today's status.

And on the second half of the question — *when do you open* — the record is worse
than it first appears. **Five of eighteen independents publish no first-party hours
at all** (Palmetto, Skyway, Riviera, Gasparilla, and CB's, whose hours are locked
inside a PNG). Island Discount Tackle publishes an open time with no close. So a
third of the set cannot answer the simpler of the two questions either — including
Skyway, which otherwise runs the best site in the audit.

### Highest-value thing that could be built

A **"today's bait" board**: one URL per shop, phone-first, that the owner updates in
about ten seconds from behind the counter — live shrimp yes/no and size, other live
bait, and today's actual hours, each with a visible "updated 6:12am" timestamp. It
embeds into whatever site the shop already has and can be pushed to a Google Business
Profile post.

It wins because it needs no rebuild, it is the only thing here that changes daily,
and the timestamp is the whole product: "updated 6:12am today" is worth more to an
angler than any amount of design.

### What a realistic engagement looks like

- **Google Business Profile cleanup** — cheapest, fastest, and it reaches the anglers
  who never open a website at all. Almost certainly the most sellable item on this
  list. It is also a one-off with no recurring revenue, and it cannot be scoped
  honestly until the GBP gap in this audit is actually closed.
- **One-page site** — genuinely warranted for Mitch's and 4th Street Tackle. Hardest
  to sell: it is the largest cheque and the least obviously urgent to the owner.
- **"Today's bait" board** — highest value to the *angler*, and the only recurring-
  revenue candidate. Hardest to sustain, for the reason below.

**Most sellable: the GBP cleanup.** Most valuable: the bait board. They are not the
same thing, and that gap is the core problem with this as a services business.

### The counter-case, honestly

The evidence points against this being a real opportunity.

**The pool is six prospects.** Eighteen independents, eleven of which have a site
that adequately does its job. Six is not a market; it is a handful of favours.

**The shops that stayed still chose to.** This is the finding that should decide it.
Fishin' Frank's updates a place-by-place fishing report *every week*, by hand, in
FrontPage, in 2026. That is not a business unaware its website is old — that is a
business that has weighed it and does not care. Island Discount Tackle's site has
been substantially unchanged since roughly 2014. 4th Street Tackle has left a TLS
certificate expired for sixteen months; renewal is free and takes minutes, and every
customer hitting a browser security warning is a visible symptom. Nobody who cared
would leave that. **An expired certificate is not a hot lead. It is evidence of a
decision.**

**The best-run shops already do the thing.** Skyway publishes dated fishing reports
every two to four days and has done all August. Economy Tackle posts through last
week. CB's runs captains' reports. These shops did not need an agency to work out
that fresh content matters — which means the pitch is not "here is something you
haven't thought of," it is "here is something your competitors already do," aimed at
the shops that have already declined to do it.

**The bait board's failure mode is worse than doing nothing.** It only works if the
owner updates it daily. These owners open at 5am and stand behind a counter until
dark. The realistic trajectory is three diligent weeks, then silence — and a stale
"live shrimp: YES" from last Tuesday actively sends anglers to a shop that has none.
That damages the shop, the angler, and the guide that pointed at it.

**The economics do not work.** Six prospects at $30–50/month, assuming every one
converts and none churn, is $200–300/month against ongoing support obligations for
daily-updated content. That is not a business.

**Recommendation.** As a services business: **a distraction.** Do not become a web
agency for six bait shops, four of which have visibly opted out already.

But there is a real product buried in the finding. The app already holds verified
hours and stock for twenty shops, `carries` and `hours` fields already exist, and
`src/lib/sponsorship.ts` already separates payment from presence. A "today's bait"
surface **inside the app** — where the guide's own audience already is — gives shops
a reason to keep their entry accurate, and it is the same value proposition sold to
the same people without taking on client work. The audit's most useful output is not
a prospect list. It is confirmation that the single question this audience asks is
answered *nowhere* across twenty businesses, which makes it worth answering in the
product.

### Before anyone is approached

Close the verification gap first. Google Business Profile presence, ratings, review
counts and recent social activity are **entirely unverified in this document**, and
those signals could easily change the ranking above — a shop with a stale site but a
well-run, heavily reviewed GBP is not underserved, it simply put its effort somewhere
else. Roughly thirty minutes of manual searching. Do it before the first call.
