# Naming and discovery

Researched 22 Aug 2026, against two questions: **what do people type into a
search box**, and **what would surface if they asked an AI instead**. Method
was live Google autocomplete (real completions in Google's own ranking), the
iTunes Search API, and RDAP registry lookups. Autocomplete shows relative
demand and phrasing, not volume.

---

## 1. How people actually ask

### The category phrase, confirmed

Searching **"shore fishing guide"** completes to:

> shore fishing guide **near me** · shore fishing guide **kauai** · **maui** ·
> **aruba** · **grand cayman** · **oahu**

That is the whole product thesis in one autocomplete list: **somebody in an
unfamiliar place who wants to fish and does not have a boat.** Every one of
those is a destination, not a hometown. It is also the expansion path — the
same phrase works for any coast we add.

**`shore fishing guide <place>` is the phrase to own.**

### The other live patterns

| Seed | What people actually type |
|---|---|
| `app to find fishing spots` | + near me · free · reddit · **best app to** |
| `where to fish near` | + me for free · me now · **sarasota** · nearby |
| `fishing without a boat` | **inshore fishing without a boat** · spots in \<city\> without a boat |
| `shoreline fishing app` | **surf fishing app** · **coastal fishing app** |

### Language nobody uses — do not write copy around it

`how to fish a new area` and `fishing in an unfamiliar place` both return
**zero completions**. The idea is right; those words are not how anyone asks
for it. People name the *place* and the *access*, never the unfamiliarity.

### Being found inside an AI prompt

Different mechanism, and it changes what the name has to do. Asked *"where can
I fish from shore near Anna Maria Island"*, a model surfaces what it has
knowledge of and can retrieve. Three things drive that, and only one is the
name:

1. **Being described in the language above**, consistently, everywhere.
   `shore fishing guide` is the phrase; use it verbatim in the store subtitle,
   the meta description, the About page and every post.
2. **Having citable content.** The 25 sourced spot pages are the asset here,
   which is another argument for fixing the SEO prerendering blocker.
3. **A name that does not collide.** A name that shares a phrase with a bigger
   entity is invisible, because the bigger entity absorbs every query.

**Implication: the name carries less discovery weight than the descriptor.**
The right structure is a distinctive name with the category permanently
attached — `Shorebound — Shore Fishing Guide`. The descriptor does the finding,
the name does the owning.

## 2. Collision testing killed three of four earlier favourites

Every name below was checked against live autocomplete. This is the step that
should have run first, and did not.

| Name | What the search engine thinks it is | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Read the Water** | *The Water Magician* — a manga/light novel dominates every completion | ☠️ dead |
| **Fish on Foot** | **"fish eye on foot"** — plantar warts, and their removal | ☠️ dead |
| **Pier to Pass** | **PierPass** — a real US port freight operator, with fee and login pages | ☠️ dead |
| Tides & Tackle | Two small bait shops. Weak, but generic and unownable | weak |
| Tideline | **Tideline Boats**, and *tideline bradenton* — in our own market | ☠️ dead |
| Shorewalk | **Shorewalk Vacation Villas, Bradenton** — our own market again | ☠️ dead |
| Saltline / Slackwater / Bankside / Shorewise / Nearwater / Waterwise | Homes, pizza, a London hotel, consultancies, *waterwise manatee* | ☠️ dead |
| Fishable | `Fishable - Fishing App` already on the App Store; .com and .fish gone | ☠️ dead |
| Shorecast | `ShoreCasts` app exists; .com and .app gone. Also *"-cast"* implies a forecast this guide refuses to make | ☠️ dead |

**Read the Water was recommended twice before this test was run.** It is the
best-sounding name on the list and it is unusable: anyone told the name and
searching it lands in a Japanese light novel.

## 3. What survives

### Shorebound — recommended

`shorebound.app` · `.fish` · `.co` · `.guide` all available.
`shorebound.com` is registered but serves a 404 — nothing is on it, so it may
be obtainable.

- Says the thing: bound to the shore. Twenty-two of twenty-five spots are
  reached on foot.
- **Expands cleanly.** Not a place, not a species, not a structure. Works on
  any coast we add.
- App Store: an unrelated app holds the name (Command B Limited, 5 ratings)
  plus *Shore Bound – Cruise Planner*. Google Play is clear for fishing.
  Names are not exclusive on either store without a trademark, but this is the
  one real cost.
- Autocomplete is diffuse — a dress, a studio, some books, and
  *"shorebound fishing alaska"*. That last one cuts both ways: it confirms the
  word is used for exactly this kind of fishing, and it means the word is not
  wholly ownable.

### Shorelark — the alternative

`shorelark.app` available; `.com` is a parked lander and probably buyable.
A real shorebird, distinctive, memorable, no meaningful collision. Weaker on
meaning — it says nothing about fishing, and would lean entirely on the
descriptor.

### Wadewise — clean but rejected

The only name tested with **zero** competing results anywhere. Rejected
anyway: "wade" describes five of twenty-five spots and excludes the piers and
bridges, so it would misdescribe the product from day one.

---

## 4. Recommendation

**Shorebound**, presented as **Shorebound — Shore Fishing Guide**.

Buy `shorebound.app` (primary), `.fish` and `.co` defensively. Ask a broker
about `shorebound.com` — a registered domain serving a 404 is often gettable.

Then use the descriptor everywhere, verbatim:

- **Store title:** Shorebound — Shore Fishing Guide
- **Subtitle / short description:** Shore fishing guide for Florida's Gulf coast
- **Meta description, About page, every post:** the same phrase
- **Per-place pages:** `Shore fishing guide — <place>`, which is the exact
  string people search and the exact string an AI is asked for

---

## 5. Before buying

Registry availability is not trademark clearance. Run a USPTO search on the
finalist and check Florida state marks — a Florida-named product in a Florida
market makes the state register worth the ten minutes.
